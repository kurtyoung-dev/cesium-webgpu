## Verdict

The day's engineering is better than its governance, and the arithmetic is better than both — I independently re-derived every load-bearing number I could reach and almost all reproduce exactly: 633 probes / median 263 / max 6,226 / 12x = 3,156 with exactly ten probes and five gate libs triggering and zero above the charter's inert ~7,000 (R-21); 16,128 B per pick and 945.0 KiB/s at 60 Hz, with 63 the unique largest ODD aperture fitting one 256-byte WebGPU row (R-22); 24 quiet-hours commits as permanent ancestors of main and 117 violations decomposing 33/37/33/14 (R-1, R-10); 614 to 2,004 catalog lines with the launcher genuinely untracked (R-8); drillPickLoop unbounded at PickingRayHelpers.js:393 (R-16). Five rulings are sound as recorded and several more have sound halves worth keeping — R-11's request/response vocabulary split, R-16's reframe that drill is not exceptional but merely first to expose an incomplete predicate, R-20's refusal of a parallel probe lane, R-24's demotion of a self-derived envelope that cannot fail, and R-25's two-commit revert boundary are all correct and well-argued. The failures cluster in one pattern: rulings that specify a verdict without checking the mechanism that must carry it. R-7 orders an enum value into a ledger whose only consumer hard-codes the two-value enum and the exact counts 20/3, walking the executor straight into charter §1.2. R-9 orders a predicate FAIL-capable against a bar that does not exist, so every reachable failure at eclipse-cloud-response-gate.mjs:2900 is an evidence-shape defect and the change converts harness errors into product regressions. R-12's standing constraint is physically unsatisfiable because .mcp.json is gitignored at .gitignore:5 and R-19's dictated `git add -A` cannot stage it, while R-19's post-commit porcelain check cannot see the failure. The single most important problem is the picking cluster's numbers: R-22 states a recoverable-rate pair (16.0°/s at k=2, 21.4°/s at k=1) that cannot both hold — the banked model is exactly linear in k so the ratio must be 2.0, and the two figures are generated by two different ε budgets (R/2 and R/3) — and neither stated figure reaches the 30°/s "typical orbit drag" the ruling claims it reaches. Worse, R-22's binding condition names three of the five coupled rectangles and omits the scissor and the cull volume, so a letter-compliant implementation widens only the copy region and yields a 63×63 buffer holding 3×3 rendered pixels inside a 60-px border of cleared zeros — whose vast uniform plateau of pick-ID 0 then "provably" serves undefined. That is a proof-carrying serve of the exact failure the architecture was ratified to eliminate.

## Pattern-consistency verdict

The day's work is largely sound and the maintainer's worked example was caught correctly by the fork's own instincts: R-16 reverses the drillPick carve-out for exactly the right reason (the predicate is incomplete, not drill exceptional), R-3 generalizes two shipped reprojection mechanisms rather than inventing a tolerance, R-23's auto-prewarm amendment moved toward the shipped `pickHoverAsync` prewarm-on-intent pattern, and R-10/R-24 apply the fork's publish-both and pre-registration conventions verbatim. Net: trust the day's engineering judgment, but nine documents now say something the rulings overturn, and five rulings need a clause or a home added before implementation starts.
